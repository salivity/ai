Understanding Causal Scrubbing in Neural Network Circuits

This article explores how causal scrubbing tests mechanistic hypotheses about the internal computational circuits of trained artificial intelligence networks. It begins by defining mechanistic interpretability and the challenge of verifying how neural networks process information. Next, it breaks down the core mechanics of the causal scrubbing algorithm, specifically focusing on activation patching and resampling ablations. Finally, it explains how researchers measure the validity of a hypothesized circuit by comparing model performance before and after these targeted interventions.

The Goal of Mechanistic Interpretability

Trained artificial intelligence networks, particularly large language models, operate as massive graphs of matrix multiplications. While they produce accurate outputs, their internal decision-making processes remain largely opaque. Mechanistic interpretability aims to reverse-engineer these networks by identifying "circuits"—specific subgraphs of neurons and attention heads that execute human-understandable algorithms to complete a task. However, proposing a plausible circuit is not enough; researchers need a rigorous, automated method to prove that the identified circuit is actually responsible for the network's behavior. Causal scrubbing provides this verification.

Defining the Computational Hypothesis

To use causal scrubbing, a researcher must first translate their informal understanding of a circuit into a formal hypothesis. This involves two components:

  1. The Computational Graph: The exact pathways (nodes and edges) inside the neural network that the researcher believes are active during a specific task.
  2. The Interpretation: A simplified mapping that defines what high-level features each node in the proposed circuit represents.

Importantly, the hypothesis also implies what does not matter. Any pathway outside the proposed circuit, and any information not captured by the high-level interpretation, is assumed to be irrelevant noise for the task at hand.

How Causal Scrubbing Works

Causal scrubbing tests a hypothesis by systematically destroying all information that the hypothesis claims is irrelevant, while preserving the information it claims is vital. It achieves this through a technique called resampling ablation.

Instead of zeroing out activations—which can push the network into an unnatural state—causal scrubbing replaces the activations of specific nodes with activations generated by entirely different inputs from the dataset. This process follows strict rules based on the hypothesis:

  • Scrubbing Unimportant Pathways: If the hypothesis states that a specific connection between Layer 1 and Layer 2 is not part of the circuit, the algorithm intercepts the activation traveling along that connection. It replaces it with an activation generated by a random input. If the pathway truly does not matter, this replacement will not harm the model's final output.
  • Testing Node Equivalence: If the hypothesis claims a specific neuron only detects whether an input noun is singular or plural, then any two inputs with plural nouns should be interchangeable at that specific neuron. Causal scrubbing tests this by swapping the neuron's activation from one plural-noun input with the activation from a completely different plural-noun input. Because both inputs share the same high-level feature (plurality), the circuit should continue to function normally despite the swap.

Evaluating the Hypothesis

The final step of causal scrubbing is measuring the network's performance (usually via its loss function) after these interventions are applied across many different inputs.

If the hypothesized circuit is accurate and complete, the "scrubbed" network will perform nearly identically to the unaltered network. The expected loss will remain low because only irrelevant activations were randomized, and only logically equivalent activations were swapped.

Conversely, if the hypothesis is incorrect—for example, if it missed a crucial attention head or misunderstood what feature a neuron actually tracks—the resampling ablations will corrupt vital information. The network's performance will degrade significantly, and the loss will spike, thereby falsifying the mechanistic hypothesis. Through this rigorous process of targeted intervention, causal scrubbing transforms interpretability from qualitative guesswork into a falsifiable science.
