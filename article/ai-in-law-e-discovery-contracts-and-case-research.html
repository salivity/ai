AI in Law: E-Discovery, Contracts, and Case Research

Artificial intelligence is transforming the legal industry by automating time-consuming tasks and increasing accuracy. This article explores how legal professionals use AI to streamline e-discovery by filtering massive datasets, accelerate contract review by identifying risks and standardizing language, and enhance case law research through natural language processing and predictive analytics.

Streamlining E-Discovery

Electronic discovery (e-discovery) requires legal teams to sort through millions of digital documents, including emails, text messages, and databases, to find evidence relevant to a lawsuit. AI drastically reduces the time and cost associated with this phase of litigation.

Legal professionals use a form of AI known as Technology Assisted Review (TAR) or predictive coding. Lawyers train the AI by reviewing a small sample of documents and marking them as relevant or irrelevant. The AI learns from these human decisions and applies that logic to the remaining millions of documents. Additionally, AI tools can:

  • Identify and group duplicate or near-duplicate files to prevent redundant review.
  • Detect patterns in communication networks to see who was talking to whom and when.
  • Automatically flag documents containing privileged information to prevent accidental disclosure to opposing counsel.

Accelerating Contract Review

Reviewing, drafting, and managing contracts is a routine yet critical function in corporate law. Doing this manually is slow and prone to human error. AI-powered contract review software acts as a first line of defense by scanning agreements in seconds and comparing them against a firm's pre-established standards.

When a legal professional uploads a contract into an AI tool, the software extracts key data points such as renewal dates, payment terms, and termination clauses. The AI then highlights areas that deviate from standard company policy or pose potential legal risks. For example, if an indemnification clause is missing or written unfavorably, the AI flags it and suggests pre-approved alternative phrasing. This allows lawyers to skip the line-by-line reading of standard boilerplate text and focus entirely on negotiating high-risk terms.

Enhancing Case Law Research

Traditionally, legal research required lawyers to use rigid keyword searches and Boolean operators to comb through vast databases of statutes and judicial opinions. If a lawyer did not use the exact terminology found in a ruling, they could easily miss a vital precedent.

AI has modernized this process through Natural Language Processing (NLP). Legal professionals can now type complex legal questions into research platforms in plain English. The AI understands the context and legal concepts behind the query, returning relevant case law even if the exact keywords do not match. Furthermore, AI tools assist in research by:

  • Generating concise summaries of lengthy court opinions.
  • Checking citations in legal briefs to ensure the cited cases have not been overturned.
  • Using predictive analytics to analyze a specific judge's past rulings, helping lawyers determine the likelihood of a motion succeeding based on historical data.
