Bio-Inspired Seal Whisker Arrays for Underwater AI Tracking

This article explores how engineered tactile arrays modeled after harbor seal whiskers enable underwater vehicles to navigate and track targets in pitch-black water. By combining specialized mechanical structures that suppress self-induced noise with advanced fluid-structure artificial intelligence, these bio-inspired sensors interpret complex hydrodynamic wakes to determine the size, speed, and direction of moving objects without relying on light or sonar.

Harbor seals can effortlessly track fish in completely dark, murky waters where vision is useless and high-frequency sonar can fail or alert prey. They achieve this using their vibrissae, or whiskers, which have a unique, wavy, elliptical cross-section. When a standard cylinder moves through water, it creates a trail of alternating vortices—a phenomenon known as Vortex-Induced Vibration (VIV)—which causes the cylinder to vibrate wildly. The undulating geometry of a seal's whisker disrupts these coherent vortex streets. This structural adaptation drastically reduces self-noise, allowing the whisker to remain still as the seal swims, making it hyper-sensitive to external water movements.

Engineers replicate this biological geometry using high-resolution 3D printing and flexible polymers to create artificial whisker arrays. At the base of each synthetic whisker, micro-electromechanical systems (MEMS), piezoelectric sensors, or strain gauges act as artificial nerve endings. As an underwater vehicle moves through the dark, these sensors measure the minute bending moments and frequencies generated when the whiskers encounter an external hydrodynamic wake. Because the whisker’s wavy shape filters out the vehicle's own movement noise, the sensors only record the distinct fluid signatures left behind by other moving objects.

While the physical array captures the mechanical data, fluid-structure artificial intelligence is required to translate these physical vibrations into actionable navigation commands. Hydrodynamic wakes are chaotic, three-dimensional fluid structures that decay over time. The AI system processes the time-series strain data coming from the array to map the fluid-structure interactions occurring around the vehicle. By analyzing the phase differences and amplitude variations across multiple whiskers simultaneously, the AI reconstructs the invisible turbulent environment in real time.

To make sense of this turbulence, researchers train deep learning models, such as Convolutional Neural Networks (CNNs) and Recurrent Neural Networks (RNNs), on massive datasets of fluid dynamics. The AI learns to recognize specific vortex shedding frequencies and pressure gradients. When the whisker array passes through a wake, the neural network identifies the exact pattern of the swirling water. From this pattern, the algorithm calculates the target's direction, its velocity, how long ago it passed, and even its general shape and size, functioning effectively as a hydrodynamic "footprint" reader.

The integration of seal-inspired tactile arrays and fluid-structure AI offers a transformative alternative to traditional underwater sensing. Unlike optical cameras, which fail in turbid water, or active sonar, which consumes significant power and reveals the vehicle's location, mechanical wake tracking is entirely passive and stealthy. Autonomous Underwater Vehicles (AUVs) equipped with these systems can silently track marine life, follow other submersibles, and navigate complex, pitch-black obstacle courses solely by feeling the movement of the surrounding water.
