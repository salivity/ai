Optimizing SAG Mills With Acoustics and AI

This article explores how modern mining concentrators utilize shell acoustic emissions and discrete-element artificial intelligence in Semi-Autogenous Grinding (SAG) mills to prevent structural liner damage and optimize the final ore particle size distribution. By combining real-time sound monitoring with advanced physics-based AI simulations, operators can accurately track internal mill dynamics, reduce maintenance downtime, and ensure efficient mineral recovery.

Understanding SAG Mill Challenges

Semi-Autogenous Grinding (SAG) mills are critical assets in mining concentrators, responsible for crushing large ore rocks into smaller particles using a mixture of the ore itself and heavy steel grinding balls. The inside of a SAG mill is a hostile, visually inaccessible environment. If the mill runs too fast or lacks enough ore inside, the steel balls will launch and strike the metal shell liners directly instead of crushing the rock. This causes catastrophic liner damage and forces expensive maintenance shutdowns. Conversely, if the grinding is inefficient, the output ore particle size distribution (PSD) becomes uneven, which drastically lowers mineral extraction rates in downstream processing.

The Role of Shell Acoustic Emissions

To monitor the blind interior of a rotating SAG mill, engineers use acoustic emission sensors mounted either on the mill shell or near the mill perimeter. As the mill turns, the colliding ore and steel balls generate specific sound frequencies and vibration patterns.

Acoustic monitoring works by isolating these sound waves:

  • Toe and Shoulder Detection: The "shoulder" is the point where the rotation lifts the rock and steel balls before they fall, and the "toe" is the bottom zone where they crash down. Acoustic sensors pinpoint the exact angles of the toe and shoulder in real time.
  • Impact Differentiation: Steel hitting steel produces a sharp, high-frequency acoustic spike, while steel hitting a bed of ore produces a dampened, lower-frequency sound.

When sensors detect the high-frequency spikes of direct steel-on-liner impacts, the system alerts operators that the ore level is too low or the rotation speed is too high.

Integrating Discrete-Element Artificial Intelligence

While acoustics tell operators what is currently happening, discrete-element artificial intelligence predicts what will happen next and determines how to respond. This technology merges the Discrete Element Method (DEM)—a physics modeling technique that simulates the movement and collision of millions of individual rocks and steel balls—with machine learning algorithms.

Historically, DEM simulations were too slow to use in real time because calculating millions of particle collisions takes vast computational power. By training artificial intelligence on thousands of pre-run DEM scenarios, the AI learns the physics of the mill. When fed live data from the acoustic sensors, the AI acts as a real-time digital twin of the SAG mill, instantly estimating internal charge volume, rock hardness, and slurry viscosity.

Preventing Liner Damage

The synergy of acoustics and AI creates an automated defense system for the mill liners. When the acoustic sensors detect an increase in steel-on-liner strikes at the toe of the charge, the AI analyzes the current operational parameters.

Instead of waiting for a human operator to notice the trend, the AI automatically calculates the safest correction. It immediately slows the mill’s rotation speed to alter the trajectory of the falling grinding balls, ensuring they land on the rock bed rather than the exposed metal liners. Alternatively, it can increase the fresh ore feed rate to build a protective cushion of rock inside the mill, extending the physical lifespan of the liners.

Optimizing Ore Particle Size Distribution

Beyond protecting the equipment, this system directly controls the quality of the ground ore. To optimize the particle size distribution, the rock must be broken down primarily by impact (falling balls crushing large rocks) and attrition (rocks rubbing against each other to create fine particles).

The discrete-element AI monitors the acoustic signals to maintain the exact charge motion required for the current ore type:

  • Adjusting for Hard Ore: If the AI detects harder ore accumulating, it subtly increases mill speed to lift the steel balls higher, generating more kinetic energy to shatter the tough rock.
  • Adjusting for Soft Ore: If the ore is soft, the AI reduces the speed to favor cascading attrition, preventing over-grinding into unusable slimes.
  • Water and Feed Control: The AI simultaneously adjusts water addition and feed rates to ensure the crushed particles exit the mill grates at the exact target size needed for downstream ball mills or flotation circuits.

By locking the mill into its optimal grinding zone, mining concentrators achieve a consistent particle size distribution, maximizing throughput while consuming less energy per ton of processed ore.
