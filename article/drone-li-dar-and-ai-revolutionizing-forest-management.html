Drone LiDAR and AI: Revolutionizing Forest Management

Forestry managers are increasingly turning to drone-mounted LiDAR and canopy segmentation artificial intelligence to modernize timber tracking and forest health monitoring. This article provides an overview of how these combined technologies replace slow, manual ground surveys with rapid, high-precision digital assessments. By capturing three-dimensional forest structures and analyzing tree crowns with machine learning, managers can accurately calculate timber volume and detect the subtle signs of early bark beetle infestations before outbreaks devastate entire landscapes.

Capturing the Forest with Drone LiDAR

Light Detection and Ranging (LiDAR) sensors mounted on Unmanned Aerial Vehicles (UAVs) emit thousands of laser pulses per second toward the forest floor. Unlike standard aerial photography, which only captures the top of the canopy, LiDAR pulses penetrate through leaves and branches to hit the ground and tree trunks below.

As these pulses bounce back to the drone, the sensor measures the return time to generate a dense, three-dimensional point cloud. This point cloud provides two critical baselines for forestry managers:

  • Digital Terrain Model (DTM): The exact topography of the bare forest floor.
  • Digital Surface Model (DSM): The top layer of the forest canopy.

By subtracting the DTM from the DSM, software generates a Canopy Height Model (CHM), which reveals the exact height of the vegetation across the surveyed area.

Canopy Segmentation AI: Isolating Individual Trees

A raw 3D point cloud is just a massive collection of data points until artificial intelligence processes it. Canopy segmentation AI uses deep learning and computer vision algorithms to analyze the Canopy Height Model and the 3D point cloud simultaneously.

The AI identifies the highest points in the canopy (tree tops) and traces the downward slope of the branches to delineate the exact boundaries of every single tree crown. This process, known as Individual Tree Detection (ITD), separates overlapping branches in dense forests, turning a continuous green blur into a database of distinct, measurable trees.

Calculating Timber Volume

Once the AI has segmented the forest into individual trees, calculating timber volume becomes a precise automated process. The system extracts specific metrics from each isolated tree:

  • Tree Height: Measured from the bare-earth DTM to the highest LiDAR point of the segmented crown.
  • Crown Diameter: The exact width and surface area of the tree's foliage.
  • Stem Location and DBH: High-density LiDAR can sometimes measure the trunk directly, or the AI can use the tree’s height and crown width to predict the Diameter at Breast Height (DBH) using established allometric equations.

By combining tree height, crown size, and species classification, the software calculates the merchantable timber volume for every tree. Forestry managers can instantly generate comprehensive inventory reports detailing total biomass, wood volume, and carbon stocks without stepping foot in the forest with a measuring tape.

Spotting Early Bark Beetle Infestations

Beyond timber inventory, this technology is vital for early pest detection. Bark beetles bore into trees and cut off their nutrient and water supply. By the time a tree's needles turn red—making the infestation visible to the human eye—the beetles have already reproduced and flown to neighboring trees.

To catch infestations during the "green attack" phase, drones are often equipped with multispectral sensors alongside LiDAR. The process works in three steps:

  1. Tree Isolation: The canopy segmentation AI uses the LiDAR data to isolate the exact crown of each individual tree, filtering out shadows, soil, and understory brush that might skew health data.
  2. Spectral Analysis: For each segmented crown, the AI analyzes multispectral reflectance data, specifically in the near-infrared and red-edge bands. A tree suffering from early beetle stress loses chlorophyll and moisture, causing a microscopic drop in near-infrared reflectance long before the needles change color to the naked eye.
  3. Structural Monitoring: LiDAR also detects subtle physical changes, such as micro-defoliation or drooping branches at the very top of the crown, which are early physical indicators of beetle stress.

Actionable Data for Rapid Intervention

When the AI flags a tree with declining chlorophyll levels and structural stress, it tags the tree with precise GPS coordinates. Forestry managers receive a digital map pinpointing the exact trees experiencing early-stage bark beetle attacks. Ground crews can then hike directly to those specific coordinates to fell and remove the infected trees, effectively halting the spread of the beetles and saving the surrounding timber inventory.
