How Top-p Sampling Cuts the Tail in Creative AI Writing

Nucleus sampling, commonly known as Top-p sampling, is a decoding strategy used in artificial intelligence to generate coherent yet creative text. This article provides an overview of how Top-p sampling works by dynamically truncating the long tail of low-probability tokens during text generation. By focusing only on a concentrated "nucleus" of likely words and adjusting the pool size based on the model's real-time confidence, Top-p sampling prevents AI from derailing into nonsensical outputs while preserving linguistic variety.

When a language model writes, it calculates a probability distribution over its entire vocabulary to decide the next token. This vocabulary often contains tens of thousands of words. While a few tokens will have high probabilities, thousands of irrelevant or bizarre tokens will have tiny, non-zero probabilities. Collectively, these thousands of unlikely options form the "unreliable tail" of the distribution. Even though each individual token in the tail has a minuscule chance of being picked, their combined probability mass is large enough that pure random sampling will eventually select one, ruining the quality of the creative writing.

Top-p sampling eliminates this unreliable tail through a Cumulative Probability Threshold. Instead of selecting from a fixed number of tokens (like Top-k sampling), the algorithm sorts all possible next tokens from highest to lowest probability. It then adds these probabilities together one by one until their cumulative sum reaches a predefined threshold, represented as the value p (for example, p = 0.90 or 90%).

Once this threshold is met, every token included in that sum forms the "nucleus." The algorithm immediately truncates—or cuts off—every single token that falls outside of this nucleus. The remaining tokens in the nucleus then have their probabilities re-normalized so that they add up to 100%, and the AI randomly samples the next word strictly from this refined group.

The defining feature of Top-p sampling is that this truncation is dynamic. The size of the nucleus expands and contracts automatically at every step of the writing process, adapting to the shape of the probability distribution:

High Uncertainty (Flat Distribution): In creative writing, when an AI starts a new sentence or describes an open-ended scene, many words could logically follow. For instance, after the phrase "The detective looked at the...", hundreds of nouns are valid. Because the probability mass is spread thinly across many options, the algorithm must include a large number of tokens to reach the 90% threshold. The nucleus expands, allowing the AI a wide, creative variety of words to choose from while still cutting off the bottom 10% of completely unrelated words.

High Confidence (Peaked Distribution): Conversely, when the context strongly dictates the next word, the probability distribution becomes very narrow. After the phrase "The heavy rain began to...", the word "fall" or "pour" might hold 95% of the probability mass on their own. To reach the 90% threshold, the algorithm only needs one or two tokens. The nucleus dynamically shrinks, and the algorithm truncates nearly the entire vocabulary. This ensures the AI does not suddenly inject a bizarre word when only a few specific words make grammatical or contextual sense.

By dynamically adjusting the number of considered tokens at each step, Top-p sampling acts as an automated safety net. It guarantees that the AI never picks from the unreliable tail of low-probability tokens, seamlessly shifting between broad creativity when the narrative allows it and strict precision when the grammar demands it.
