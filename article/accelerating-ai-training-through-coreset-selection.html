Accelerating AI Training Through Coreset Selection

Coreset selection is a data-centric optimization technique that accelerates artificial intelligence training by identifying and extracting the most informative subset of a massive dataset. Rather than feeding millions of redundant data points into a neural network, coreset algorithms analyze the data's underlying geometry, training dynamics, and gradient contributions to curate a compact, representative sample. This article explores the core mechanisms behind coreset selection—specifically geometry-based, uncertainty-based, and gradient-matching methods—that enable machine learning models to achieve competitive accuracy in a fraction of the time and computational cost.

Geometry-Based Selection

Geometry-based methods operate on the assumption that data points close to one another in the feature space share similar information. To eliminate redundancy, these algorithms map the entire dataset into a high-dimensional space and select a subset that best covers the overall distribution.

By utilizing clustering techniques like k-means or k-center, the algorithm identifies central data points (centroids) that represent their surrounding neighbors. Training the AI model exclusively on these representative centroids ensures the model learns the global structure of the data without wasting compute power on nearly identical samples.

Uncertainty and Error-Based Selection

Not all data points are equally difficult for a model to learn. Uncertainty-based methods identify the most informative samples by measuring how much a model struggles to classify them during early training stages.

This approach tracks specific metrics, such as:

  • Predictive Entropy: Selecting data points where the model's prediction confidence is low.
  • Decision Boundary Proximity: Isolating samples that lie closest to the model's decision boundaries, as these points are critical for defining accurate classifications.
  • Forgetting Events: Tracking how often a data point is correctly classified in one training epoch and misclassified in the next. Samples that are frequently "forgotten" contain complex patterns essential for robust training, while easily learned samples are discarded.

Gradient-Matching Algorithms

Artificial intelligence models learn by updating their internal weights using gradients, which indicate the direction and magnitude of error correction needed during training. Gradient-matching is one of the most direct and effective coreset selection techniques.

Instead of looking at the data points themselves, this method looks at how the data affects the model. The algorithm searches for a small, weighted subset of data whose combined gradients closely approximate the average gradient of the entire massive dataset. If the subset pushes the model's weights in the exact same direction as the full dataset would, the model will converge to the same level of accuracy while processing only a fraction of the data.

Submodular Optimization

To balance diversity and representativeness, many coreset frameworks use submodular functions. Submodularity is a mathematical property analogous to diminishing returns: adding a new data point to a small subset provides a large amount of new information, while adding that same point to a massive subset provides very little.

Algorithms use submodular optimization to greedily select data points that maximize information gain at each step. Once the information gain hits a predetermined threshold of diminishing returns, the selection process stops, resulting in an optimal, minimal dataset ready for rapid AI training.
