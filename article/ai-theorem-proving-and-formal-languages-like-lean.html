AI Theorem Proving and Formal Languages Like Lean

This article explores how artificial intelligence achieves automated theorem proving by leveraging formal languages such as Lean to construct and strictly verify mathematical proofs. It covers the fundamental role of formal verification, the mechanics of the Lean theorem prover, the integration of machine learning to navigate complex proof searches, and how this synergy eliminates AI hallucinations to guarantee absolute mathematical certainty.

The Role of Formal Languages in Mathematics

Traditional mathematical proofs are written in natural language. While understandable to humans, natural language is often ambiguous and relies on intuitive leaps, making it difficult for computers to verify. Formal languages bridge this gap by translating mathematical statements into precise, unambiguous code based on strict logical foundations, such as dependent type theory.

Lean is an interactive theorem prover and programming language designed specifically for this purpose. In Lean, every mathematical object, theorem, and proof step is represented as a specific data type. To prove a theorem in Lean, a user—or an AI—must construct a valid program that matches the data type of the theorem.

How Lean Verifies Proofs

Lean relies on a small, heavily audited core component called the kernel. The kernel's only job is to check whether a sequence of logical steps abides by the foundational rules of mathematics.

To interact with the kernel, Lean uses "tactics." Tactics are instructions that transform a complex mathematical goal into simpler sub-goals. When a proof is being constructed, Lean operates as an environment with strict rules:

  1. State Representation: Lean displays the current "proof state," which includes the target goal to be proven and the known hypotheses.
  2. Tactic Application: A tactic is applied to manipulate the goal (e.g., applying a known theorem, simplifying an algebraic expression, or using induction).
  3. Verification: Lean's kernel instantly checks the validity of the tactic. If the tactic is logically sound, Lean updates the proof state. If the tactic violates a mathematical rule, Lean rejects it and returns an error.

The proof is only complete when the tactics successfully reduce all sub-goals to basic, indisputable axioms.

Integrating AI with Lean

While Lean can verify if a proof is correct, it cannot inherently figure out which tactics to use for complex theorems. The number of possible tactic combinations is practically infinite, creating a massive search space. This is where artificial intelligence, particularly deep learning and large language models (LLMs), enters the process.

AI models treat theorem proving in Lean much like playing a game of chess. The current proof state is the board, the tactics are the legal moves, and winning the game means reducing the proof state to "no goals remaining."

The AI and the Lean environment interact in a continuous loop:

  • Observation: The AI reads the current proof state generated by Lean.
  • Prediction: Using neural networks trained on existing mathematical libraries (like Lean’s mathlib), the AI predicts the most promising next tactic.
  • Execution: The AI sends the chosen tactic to Lean.
  • Feedback: Lean executes the tactic. If the move is valid, Lean presents the new proof state to the AI. If the move is invalid, Lean rejects it, and the AI must backtrack and try a different tactic using algorithms like Monte Carlo Tree Search (MCTS).

Eliminating AI Hallucinations

A major flaw of modern generative AI is its tendency to "hallucinate"—generating text that sounds confident and plausible but is factually or logically incorrect. In mathematics, a single flawed step invalidates an entire proof.

By forcing the AI to communicate its mathematical reasoning through Lean, hallucinations are rendered harmless. The AI is free to guess and generate millions of incorrect or nonsensical tactics, because Lean’s kernel acts as an incorruptible gatekeeper. It filters out every hallucinated or logically flawed step in real time. Once the AI finds a sequence of tactics that the Lean kernel accepts from start to finish, the resulting proof is guaranteed to be 100% mathematically correct, without requiring human peer review to verify its validity.
