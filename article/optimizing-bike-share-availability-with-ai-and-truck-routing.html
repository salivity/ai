Optimizing Bike-Share Availability with AI and Truck Routing

Modern bicycle-share and micromobility systems rely on spatiotemporal artificial intelligence and dynamic truck routing to prevent stations from becoming completely empty or completely full during peak commuter hours. This article explores how operators use historical data, real-time sensors, predictive algorithms, and fleet optimization to forecast commuter demand and guide redistribution vehicles, ensuring riders always have access to both bikes and open docks.

Understanding Spatiotemporal Demand

The core challenge of any dock-based micromobility network is asymmetrical commuter flow. In the morning, riders typically take bikes from residential areas and dock them in commercial districts. In the evening, this pattern reverses. Without intervention, residential docks quickly empty in the morning while downtown stations overflow, leaving riders unable to end their trips.

To solve this, operators use spatiotemporal AI—models that analyze data across both space (station locations) and time (hours, days, and seasons). These systems ingest vast amounts of data, including:

  • Historical trip records and station usage patterns.
  • Real-time weather forecasts, as rain or extreme temperatures drastically alter ridership.
  • Local events, public transit disruptions, and holidays.
  • Topographical data, since riders frequently take bikes downhill but rarely ride them back uphill.

Predictive AI for Station Rebalancing

Spatiotemporal AI uses machine learning techniques, such as Graph Neural Networks (GNNs) and Long Short-Term Memory (LSTM) networks, to treat the entire city's bike-share system as an interconnected web. Rather than looking at a single station in isolation, the AI predicts how a change in one neighborhood will affect stations miles away within the next 30 to 120 minutes.

The AI calculates a "target inventory" for every station prior to the morning and evening rush hours. For example, a high-demand residential station might need to be 90% full at 7:00 AM, while a downtown station near a major train hub might need to be 80% empty at the same time to absorb the incoming wave of commuters.

Dynamic Truck Routing and Fleet Optimization

Once the AI determines which stations will soon fail (either by hitting zero bikes or zero open docks), it passes these predictions to a routing algorithm. Operators employ fleets of vans and flatbed trucks to physically move bicycles from saturated stations to depleted ones—a process known as rebalancing.

Simply sending a truck to an empty station is inefficient. Instead, routing algorithms solve a complex variation of the Vehicle Routing Problem (VRP). The software generates dynamic, turn-by-turn routes for drivers based on several constraints:

  • Urgency: Prioritizing stations that are predicted to empty out or overflow within the next 20 minutes.
  • Truck Capacity: Ensuring a truck has enough space to pick up excess bikes before heading to a station that needs a drop-off.
  • Traffic Conditions: Utilizing real-time traffic data to prevent rebalancing trucks from getting stuck in the very commuter gridlock they are trying to alleviate.
  • Labor Efficiency: Minimizing the time drivers spend driving empty or waiting at stations.

Real-Time Adjustments During Peak Hours

As the rush hour unfolds, the system operates in a continuous feedback loop. IoT sensors in the docks and GPS units on the bikes feed live status updates back to the central AI.

If an unexpected subway delay causes a sudden surge in bike rentals at a specific station, the AI recalculates the citywide supply-and-demand matrix in real time. It immediately updates the manifest on the nearest truck driver's tablet, rerouting them to intercept the shortage before the station completely empties.

Incentivizing Rider-Based Rebalancing

To reduce the reliance on heavy trucks, spatiotemporal AI also powers user-facing incentives. When the algorithm predicts a station will soon be full or empty, the operator's mobile app can automatically offer credits, discounts, or points to riders who voluntarily pick up a bike from a crowded station or dock their bike at a depleted one just a few blocks away. By combining predictive truck routing with crowd-sourced rider rebalancing, operators maintain a balanced network throughout the heaviest commuter peaks.
