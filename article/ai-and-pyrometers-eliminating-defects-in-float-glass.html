AI and Pyrometers: Eliminating Defects in Float Glass

Float glass manufacturing requires extreme precision to prevent microscopic flaws from ruining vast ribbons of glass. This article explores how modern glass plants combine crown pyrometer arrays and defect-classification artificial intelligence (AI) to detect and eliminate two of the most common production flaws: gas bubble "seeds" and solid refractory "stones." By linking real-time thermal mapping of the furnace with automated optical inspections at the cold end, manufacturers create a closed-loop system that optimizes furnace temperatures on the fly, saves energy, and ensures crystal-clear float glass sheets.

Understanding the Defects: Seeds and Stones

Before looking at the technology, it is essential to understand the two primary defects that plague float glass production:

  • Gas Bubble Seeds: "Seeds" are tiny gas bubbles trapped within the glass ribbon. They occur during the melting and refining stages when gases released from the raw batch materials fail to rise to the surface and escape. This is almost always caused by improper viscosity due to localized cold spots in the furnace.
  • Refractory Stones: "Stones" are solid inclusions of unmelted batch material or, more commonly, tiny pieces of the furnace's own ceramic refractory bricks that have eroded and fallen into the molten glass. Refractory erosion is primarily driven by localized overheating and aggressive thermal currents within the glass melt.

The Role of Crown Pyrometer Arrays

The "crown" is the arched roof of the glass melting furnace, subjected to temperatures exceeding 1,600°C (2,912°F). Because sensors cannot survive long-term immersion directly in the molten glass, manufacturers install arrays of non-contact infrared pyrometers along the exterior looking into the crown.

These crown pyrometer arrays continuously measure the thermal radiation inside the furnace at multiple zones—from the raw material doghouse to the refining zone. By aggregating data from dozens of pyrometers, operators generate a real-time, 3D thermal map of the furnace interior.

Maintaining the exact right temperature gradient is a delicate balancing act. If the refining zone is too cool, gas bubbles cannot escape, resulting in seeds. If the crown gets too hot, the refractory bricks degrade and drop into the melt, causing stones. The pyrometer array acts as the frontline defense, ensuring the thermal profile stays within strict operational boundaries.

Defect-Classification AI at the Cold End

Even with precise thermal monitoring, microscopic defects still occur. Historically, identifying where a defect originated required taking a flawed piece of glass to a lab for chemical analysis—a process taking hours or days, during which tons of defective glass were still being produced. Today, defect-classification AI solves this in milliseconds.

As the continuous ribbon of glass cools and moves down the production line, it passes under high-speed, high-resolution optical scanners. When a scanner detects an anomaly, the AI analyzes the image to classify the defect based on light refraction, shape, and core density.

  • Identifying Seeds: The AI recognizes the smooth, spherical, or elongated nature of gas bubbles and measures their depth within the glass sheet.
  • Identifying Stones: The AI detects the irregular, opaque core of a refractory stone and can even analyze the surrounding stress halo in the glass to determine which specific section of the furnace the brick fell from.

Closing the Loop: Connecting AI to the Furnace

The true breakthrough in modern glass manufacturing is the integration of the cold-end AI with the hot-end pyrometer arrays. This creates an automated, closed-loop feedback system.

  1. Detection and Diagnosis: The AI detects a sudden uptick in a specific defect, such as micro-seeds, and instantly classifies them.
  2. Root-Cause Correlation: The AI software looks back at the historical thermal data logged by the crown pyrometer array from several hours prior (accounting for the time it takes molten glass to travel through the furnace and down the line).
  3. Automated Adjustment: If the AI sees a spike in seeds, it instructs the furnace control system to slightly increase the fuel-to-air ratio in the specific refining zone burners to raise the temperature, lowering the glass viscosity so bubbles can escape. Conversely, if the AI detects refractory stones, it alerts the system to cool down specific crown zones where pyrometers indicate localized overheating, halting further brick erosion.

By uniting the thermal oversight of crown pyrometer arrays with the diagnostic speed of defect-classification AI, float glass manufacturers have transitioned from reactive troubleshooting to proactive, self-correcting production.
