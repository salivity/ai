AI and Mortality Trends in Annuity and PRT Pricing

Actuarial longevity researchers are transforming the valuation of pension risk transfers (PRTs) and annuities by merging cause-of-death mortality improvement trends with socio-economic artificial intelligence. This article provides an overview of how breaking down mortality data by specific medical causes, processing granular demographic variables through machine learning, and combining these insights allows insurers to predict human lifespans with unprecedented accuracy and price long-term retirement liabilities effectively.

Analyzing Cause-of-Death Mortality Trends

Historically, actuaries projected future life expectancy by looking at aggregate mortality rates and extrapolating past trends into the future. Today, researchers disaggregate this data to study specific causes of death, such as cardiovascular disease, cancer, respiratory illnesses, and dementia.

By isolating these categories, actuaries can track how medical advancements, lifestyle shifts, and public health policies impact each disease individually. For example, while mortality improvements in heart disease have slowed in recent years, rapid advancements in oncology are driving new reductions in cancer mortality. Modeling these distinct trajectories prevents actuaries from misjudging overall life expectancy. It allows them to map out precisely how long a specific cohort of retirees is likely to live based on the current medical landscape.

Leveraging Socio-Economic AI

Medical trends do not affect all populations equally; longevity is heavily influenced by socio-economic status. To capture this variance, actuarial researchers use artificial intelligence and machine learning algorithms to analyze massive, complex datasets that traditional statistical models cannot easily handle.

AI models ingest traditional factors like age and gender alongside granular socio-economic indicators, including:

  • Postal codes and neighborhood deprivation indices
  • Educational attainment and occupation history
  • Income levels and net worth
  • Access to healthcare facilities and local pollution levels

Machine learning techniques, such as gradient boosting and random forests, identify non-linear relationships and hidden correlations within this data. For instance, AI can quantify the exact longevity gap between an affluent retiree living in a metropolitan area with top-tier oncology centers and a blue-collar retiree in a rural area with high rates of respiratory illness.

Pricing Pension Risk Transfers and Annuities

The ultimate goal of combining cause-of-death trends with socio-economic AI is to achieve hyper-accurate pricing for annuities and pension risk transfers. When an insurer takes on a pension plan's liabilities (a PRT) or sells an individual annuity, they are assuming longevity risk—the risk that the policyholders will live longer than expected, requiring more payouts.

Researchers apply their AI-driven, cause-specific models directly to the census data of the pension plan or annuity applicant pool:

  1. Micro-Segmentation: AI profiles the exact socio-economic makeup of the pension members.
  2. Targeted Trend Application: Actuaries overlay cause-of-death improvement trends onto those specific profiles. A wealthy cohort might be assigned higher mortality improvement rates for cancer due to better access to early detection and emerging therapies.
  3. Cash Flow Projection: The combined model projects future survival rates and calculates the exact timeline of future benefit payouts.

This precision eliminates the need for overly conservative, broad-brush risk margins. If an insurer knows a specific blue-collar pension pool has lower expected longevity improvements, they can offer a more competitive, lower price to win the PRT deal without sacrificing profitability. Conversely, if the data reveals a population poised to benefit heavily from upcoming medical breakthroughs, the insurer can price the annuity higher to ensure their capital reserves remain solvent for decades to come.
