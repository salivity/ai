How Named Entity Recognition Extracts Structured Data

Named Entity Recognition (NER) is a core component of natural language processing in artificial intelligence that transforms unstructured text into structured data. This article explores the exact process NER systems use to read raw documents, identify key concepts, and categorize them into predefined groups like names, organizations, locations, and dates. By moving from basic text preparation to advanced deep learning contextual analysis, NER bridges the gap between human language and machine-readable databases.

Text Preprocessing

Before an AI can identify entities, it must break down the unstructured document into manageable pieces through preprocessing:

  • Sentence Segmentation: The system divides the raw text into individual sentences based on punctuation and formatting.
  • Tokenization: Each sentence is split into smaller units called tokens, which are usually individual words, numbers, or punctuation marks.
  • Part-of-Speech (POS) Tagging: The AI assigns grammatical labels to each token, identifying whether a word is a noun, verb, adjective, or proper noun. Since entities are almost always nouns or proper nouns, this step significantly narrows down the search.

Entity Identification and Chunking

Once the text is tokenized, the NER system must determine the boundaries of an entity. Many entities consist of multiple words, such as "New York City" or "Bank of America."

To handle this, AI models often use a tagging scheme called IOB (Inside, Outside, Beginning). The system scans the tokens sequentially:

  • B (Beginning): Marks the first token of a detected entity.
  • I (Inside): Marks subsequent tokens that are part of the same entity.
  • O (Outside): Marks tokens that do not belong to any entity.

This chunking process ensures that multi-word phrases are extracted as a single unit rather than separate, disconnected pieces of data.

Contextual Classification

After identifying the boundaries of an entity, the system classifies it into a specific category (e.g., Person, Organization, Location, Time). Modern AI relies on statistical models and deep learning to solve the problem of ambiguity, as the same word can have different meanings depending on how it is used.

For example, the word "Apple" could refer to a fruit or a technology company. The word "Washington" could refer to a person, a state, or a city. Modern NER systems use Transformer-based architectures (like BERT or GPT) to analyze the surrounding words—the context. If the sentence reads, "Apple released a new phone," the AI uses the words "released" and "phone" to calculate a high probability that "Apple" is an Organization.

Structuring the Output

In the final stage, the NER system extracts the tagged and classified tokens from the original text and formats them into a structured layout, such as JSON, XML, or a relational database table.

For instance, the unstructured sentence, "John Smith signed the contract in London on Tuesday," is automatically converted into structured key-value pairs:

  • Person: John Smith
  • Location: London
  • Date: Tuesday

This structured output can now be easily searched, analyzed, and integrated into other software systems without human intervention.
