Protecting Heritage Trees with Sonic Tomography and AI

Cemetery and urban parkland managers face the critical challenge of preserving ancient, culturally significant trees while ensuring public safety on busy walkways. By combining tree-stability sonic tomography with wind-throw risk artificial intelligence, managers can now see inside aging tree trunks and accurately predict how they will react to severe weather. This non-invasive approach identifies internal decay and structural weaknesses long before catastrophic branch failure or uprooting occurs, allowing for targeted maintenance rather than preventative tree removal.

How Sonic Tomography Maps Internal Decay

Historically, arborists had to drill into trees to check for hollow centers, a process that could inadvertently spread fungi and bacteria. Sonic tomography eliminates this risk by using sound waves to create an internal map of the trunk.

Managers place a series of sensors around the circumference of the heritage tree. Each sensor is tapped sequentially, sending sound waves through the wood. Because sound travels much faster through dense, healthy wood than it does through decayed or hollow areas, the system can measure the exact velocity of the waves. Specialized software then converts these speed measurements into a color-coded, two-dimensional cross-section of the trunk, instantly revealing hidden cavities, rot, and structural cracks.

Predicting Failure with Wind-Throw Risk AI

Knowing a tree is hollow is only half the battle; many hollow trees remain standing for decades. To determine if a compromised tree actually poses a danger to pedestrians, managers feed the tomography data into wind-throw risk artificial intelligence platforms.

The AI combines the internal decay map with external variables to run complex biomechanical simulations. The software analyzes:

  • Canopy Architecture: Laser scanning (LiDAR) measures the weight, asymmetry, and wind-sail area of the tree's upper branches.
  • Local Wind Patterns: Historical weather data and topography help the AI model how gusts tunnel through specific cemetery corridors or park paths.
  • Soil Conditions: Moisture levels and root-zone compaction are factored in to assess anchoring strength.

By cross-referencing the remaining wall thickness of the hollow trunk against simulated storm forces, the AI calculates the exact wind speed required to snap a specific branch or uproot the entire tree.

Proactive Management Near Walkways

Armed with precise data from both the tomograph and the AI, parkland and cemetery managers can make surgical interventions to protect walkways. Instead of cutting down a beloved 200-year-old oak out of caution, arborists can use the AI models to see how much weight needs to be removed from the canopy to make the tree safe.

If a massive branch overhanging a footpath is flagged as a high failure risk, managers can selectively prune that specific limb to reduce wind drag, or install dynamic cabling systems to support the weakened junction. In cases where the AI predicts imminent failure that pruning cannot fix, managers can temporarily reroute pedestrian traffic during high-wind advisories or safely remove the tree before tragedy strikes. This technology ultimately extends the lifespan of urban canopies while keeping the public out of harm's way.
