AI Warp Control and Moisture Sensors in Corrugated Plants

This article explores how modern corrugated cardboard box plants utilize starch-glue moisture sensors and warp-control artificial intelligence (AI) to maintain flat board production on high-speed corrugators, regardless of fluctuations in ambient humidity. By integrating real-time moisture monitoring with predictive machine learning algorithms, manufacturers can automatically adjust glue application, heat, and paper tension to prevent warp, reduce waste, and ensure consistent runnability.

The Challenge of Ambient Humidity and Warp

Corrugated board is made by bonding a fluted inner medium to flat linerboards using a water-based starch adhesive. Because paper is hygroscopic, it constantly absorbs and releases moisture based on the surrounding environment. When ambient humidity shifts in the manufacturing plant, the moisture content of the paper rolls changes.

If one linerboard absorbs more moisture than the other, it expands. During the heated curing process on the corrugator, uneven moisture distribution causes uneven shrinkage. This results in "warp"—where the finished cardboard sheets bend upward, downward, or twist sideways. Warped boards jam downstream converting machinery, halt production, and generate massive material waste. On high-speed corrugators running at over 1,000 feet per minute, human operators cannot react fast enough to manually correct these micro-climatic shifts.

The Role of Starch-Glue Moisture Sensors

Starch adhesive is the primary source of added moisture during the corrugating process. To control warp, plants must first control the exact amount of moisture introduced by the glue.

Advanced non-contact moisture sensors—typically using near-infrared (NIR) or microwave technology—are installed at critical points along the corrugator:

  • Before the glue machine: Sensors measure the baseline moisture and temperature of the incoming paper liners and medium.
  • At the glue application stations: Sensors monitor the viscosity, temperature, and water ratio of the starch adhesive in real time.
  • After the double-backer (heating section): Sensors read the final moisture profile of the bonded board before it is cut.

By measuring the exact moisture level of the paper entering the machine, the system knows how much water the paper is already holding due to ambient humidity. The sensors then ensure the glue applicator applies the precise millimeter-thin film of starch needed to create a bond without over-wetting the paper.

How Warp-Control AI Manages the Corrugator

While moisture sensors gather data, warp-control AI acts as the brain of the operation. The AI system collects data from the moisture sensors, ambient plant hygrometers, infrared thermal cameras, and laser-based warp detection scanners located at the dry end of the corrugator.

When the AI detects a change in ambient humidity or sees the early stages of warp forming, it executes immediate, closed-loop adjustments across the machine without human intervention:

  1. Gap Adjustment: The AI alters the gap between the metering roll and the applicator roll by fractions of a millimeter, instantly increasing or decreasing the amount of wet starch applied to the flute tips.
  2. Thermal Profiling: The AI adjusts the steam pressure inside the hotplates and preheater drums. If a liner is too wet from high ambient humidity, the AI increases the wrap arm around the preheater drum to evaporate excess moisture before gluing.
  3. Tension Control: Wet paper stretches more easily than dry paper. The AI dynamically adjusts the braking tension on the roll stands to ensure both the top and bottom liners enter the bonding phase at equal tension.
  4. Moisture Sprays: If ambient humidity drops rapidly and makes one paper liner too dry compared to the other, the AI activates precision misting sprays to re-moisten the dry liner, balancing the sheet.

Predictive Adaptation Through Machine Learning

Unlike traditional automated systems that only react after warped boards are produced, AI uses machine learning to predict warp before it happens. The AI logs historical data regarding specific paper grades, paper mill suppliers, glue recipes, and seasonal weather patterns.

When a new paper roll is spliced into the machine, the AI cross-references the current ambient humidity with historical data for that specific paper grade. It adjusts the glue gaps, preheaters, and tension settings before the new paper even reaches the glue station. This predictive capability ensures that high-speed corrugators can transition seamlessly through weather changes, shifts in plant ventilation, and varying paper qualities while producing flat, defect-free corrugated board.
