AI and EGT Margins in On-Wing Turbofan Maintenance

Aircraft engine manufacturers optimize on-wing turbofan maintenance by combining Exhaust Gas Temperature (EGT) margin degradation tracking with artificial intelligence-powered borescope inspections. While EGT margin analysis monitors macroscopic thermodynamic efficiency to predict when an engine is approaching its operational thermal limits, computer vision AI analyzes internal videos of compressor and turbine blades to detect microscopic structural defects. By fusing this continuous sensor telemetry with automated visual diagnostics, manufacturers can accurately forecast component lifespans, prevent unscheduled groundings, and schedule minimally invasive on-wing repairs before catastrophic failures occur.

Tracking Exhaust Gas Temperature Margin Degradation

The Exhaust Gas Temperature (EGT) margin is the primary indicator of a turbofan engine's overall thermodynamic health. It represents the difference between the engine’s certified maximum permissible EGT and the actual peak EGT recorded during takeoff, when thrust and thermal stress are highest.

As an engine accumulates flight cycles, internal components naturally degrade:

  • Fan and compressor blades experience erosion from intake debris.
  • Turbine blade tips rub against outer shrouds, increasing tip clearances.
  • Air seals wear down, causing pressurized air to leak.

To compensate for this loss of aerodynamic efficiency and still produce the required takeoff thrust, the engine's control system must burn more fuel. Burning more fuel increases the temperature inside the core, steadily shrinking the EGT margin.

Manufacturers track this degradation using aircraft communications systems that transmit flight data in real time. Predictive algorithms normalize this data—accounting for ambient outside air temperature, altitude, and barometric pressure—to establish a true degradation trend line. When the smoothed data shows the EGT margin approaching zero, manufacturers know the engine is losing efficiency and requires an intervention, such as an on-wing water wash to remove compressor fouling or a targeted module repair.

Borescope Blade-Crack Vision Artificial Intelligence

While EGT margins reveal that an engine is degrading, they do not always pinpoint which specific internal part is failing. To look inside the engine without removing it from the aircraft wing, technicians thread a fiber-optic camera called a borescope through access ports to inspect the high-pressure compressor and turbine blades.

Traditionally, human inspectors watched hours of borescope video footage to manually spot defects—a slow process prone to human fatigue and subjective interpretation. Today, manufacturers route this video feed directly through deep learning computer vision models.

These AI models are trained on hundreds of thousands of historical images of engine anomalies. As the borescope rotates the engine's internal spools, the vision AI analyzes the footage frame by frame in real time to:

  • Identify hairline thermal fatigue cracks, foreign object damage (FOD) nicks, and sulfidation corrosion.
  • Distinguish between harmless surface smudges and structural fractures.
  • Automatically measure the exact length, width, and location of a crack relative to the blade root or tip.
  • Compare current defect measurements against strict, manufacturer-defined Aircraft Maintenance Manual (AMM) limits.

Fusing Data to Schedule On-Wing Maintenance

Engine manufacturers achieve peak scheduling efficiency by combining EGT telemetry with borescope AI metrics inside a digital twin of the specific engine serial number.

The scheduling workflow operates as a unified feedback loop:

  1. Thermodynamic Trigger: A steeper-than-normal drop in the EGT margin alerts the manufacturer's fleet monitoring center that an engine is experiencing accelerated wear.
  2. Targeted AI Inspection: Instead of scheduling a blind, time-consuming teardown, the manufacturer instructs line maintenance to perform a borescope inspection in the specific engine stage indicated by the thermal data.
  3. Automated Triage: The borescope vision AI detects a crack on a high-pressure turbine blade and measures it as within safe flying limits, but growing.
  4. Predictive Scheduling: Using the EGT data to calculate future thermal stress and the AI's precise measurement of the crack, predictive software calculates the exact number of flight cycles remaining before the crack reaches its critical limit.

With this combined data, manufacturers and airlines can schedule on-wing maintenance during a routine overnight layover weeks in advance. Technicians can perform an on-wing blade blending repair (grinding out small nicks) or replace specific blades through access panels, restoring the EGT margin and structural integrity without the massive expense of removing the engine from the wing.
