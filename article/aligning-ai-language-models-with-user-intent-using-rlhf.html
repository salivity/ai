Aligning AI Language Models with User Intent Using RLHF

This article provides a comprehensive overview of Reinforcement Learning from Human Feedback (RLHF) and explains how it is used to align artificial intelligence language models with human user intent. Raw AI models are initially trained merely to predict the next word in a sentence, which often results in unhelpful, inaccurate, or toxic outputs. By going through the three core stages of RLHF—Supervised Fine-Tuning, Reward Model training, and Reinforcement Learning optimization—developers can successfully steer these models to become helpful, honest, and harmless assistants.

The Challenge of Unaligned Language Models

Base large language models (LLMs) are trained on massive datasets of internet text to predict the next word in a sequence. While this makes them exceptional at recognizing patterns and generating human-like text, it does not mean they understand what a user actually wants. If you ask an unaligned model a question, it might respond with more questions, fabricate facts, or generate harmful content simply because those patterns exist in its training data. Aligning the model means bridging the gap between raw text prediction and fulfilling actual user intent.

Step 1: Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT)

The first phase of RLHF establishes a baseline of good behavior. Developers hire human annotators to write high-quality responses to a wide variety of prompts. This curated dataset demonstrates how the AI should ideally respond to instructions, questions, and creative tasks. The base language model is then trained on this data. While Supervised Fine-Tuning successfully teaches the model the correct format of a helpful assistant, it is too slow and expensive to cover every possible scenario a user might present.

Step 2: Training the Reward Model

To scale human feedback without needing humans to write every single response, developers create a separate AI called a Reward Model. The SFT model is given a single prompt and asked to generate several different responses. Human evaluators then read these responses and rank them from best to worst based on helpfulness, accuracy, and safety.

This ranking data is used to train the Reward Model. Over time, the Reward Model learns to mimic human preferences. It becomes capable of reading any prompt and response pair and assigning a numerical score that represents how much a human would approve of that specific output.

Step 3: Optimization via Reinforcement Learning

In the final stage, the language model is trained using an optimization algorithm, most commonly Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO). The process works in a continuous loop:

  1. The language model is given a new prompt and generates a response.
  2. The Reward Model evaluates the response and assigns it a score.
  3. The language model updates its internal parameters to maximize this score in future attempts.

To prevent the language model from gaming the system—such as generating nonsensical text that accidentally tricks the Reward Model into giving a high score—a mathematical penalty is applied if the model strays too far from its original Supervised Fine-Tuning baseline.

The Impact on User Intent

Through thousands of iterations in the reinforcement learning loop, the AI internalizes human preferences. Instead of just predicting the most statistically likely next word, the model optimizes its word choices to achieve the highest possible reward score. Because that reward score is directly tied to human rankings, the AI naturally shifts toward generating answers that are direct, polite, safe, and closely aligned with what the human user actually intended to achieve.
