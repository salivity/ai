AI Tariff Classification and Graphs in Export Compliance

International trade compliance teams face immense pressure to prevent dual-use goods from reaching sanctioned individuals and organizations. To manage this risk, modern compliance departments combine Harmonized System (HS) tariff classification artificial intelligence with entity-resolution graphs. This article explores how these two technologies work together to automatically identify controlled dual-use items, uncover hidden corporate ownership structures, and block illegal exports to sanctioned beneficial owners before shipments leave the warehouse.

AI-Driven Harmonized System Classification

The first step in screening exports is knowing exactly what is being shipped. Dual-use goods are products and technologies normally used for civilian purposes that may also have military applications.

Traditionally, assigning Harmonized System (HS) codes to products was a manual, error-prone process. Today, compliance teams use artificial intelligence, specifically Natural Language Processing (NLP) and machine learning, to automate this task.

The AI analyzes unstructured product data, such as technical specifications, engineering blueprints, and commercial invoices. It then cross-references this data against global tariff schedules and export control lists, such as the Commerce Control List (CCL). By accurately assigning HS codes and Export Control Classification Numbers (ECCNs) in real time, the AI flags any product that has potential dual-use capabilities, ensuring it undergoes stricter scrutiny before export.

Uncovering Ownership with Entity-Resolution Graphs

Knowing a product is dual-use is only half the battle; compliance teams must also know who is buying it. Sanctioned individuals frequently hide behind complex networks of shell companies, joint ventures, and aliases to bypass standard screening lists. Under regulations like the OFAC 50 Percent Rule, if a sanctioned person owns 50 percent or more of a company, that company is also sanctioned, even if it is not explicitly named on a government watchlist.

Entity-resolution graphs solve this problem by mapping out global corporate relationships. Using graph database technology, compliance software ingests vast amounts of data from corporate registries, shipping logs, financial records, and leaked offshore documents.

The entity-resolution algorithm cleans and links this data, recognizing when two slightly different names or addresses actually refer to the same entity. It then builds a visual network of nodes (people and companies) and edges (relationships like ownership or board membership). This allows compliance teams to trace the ultimate beneficial ownership (UBO) of a buyer across multiple jurisdictions in seconds.

Integrating AI and Graphs for Dual-Use Screening

To effectively screen exports, trade compliance teams integrate HS classification AI and entity-resolution graphs into a single, automated workflow within their Enterprise Resource Planning (ERP) systems. The process follows four distinct steps:

  1. Product Flagging: When an order is placed, the classification AI scans the bill of materials and assigns the correct HS and ECCN codes. If the item is identified as dual-use, the system triggers an enhanced screening protocol.
  2. Buyer Resolution: Simultaneously, the entity-resolution graph analyzes the purchasing company, the freight forwarder, and the end-user. It resolves their identities against global corporate records to map out their ownership structures.
  3. Sanctions Calculation: The graph calculates the aggregate ownership percentages of the buying entity's parent companies and shareholders. It checks every connected party against global sanctions lists, including the OFAC Specially Designated Nationals (SDN) list and the EU Consolidated List.
  4. Automated Transaction Hold: If a dual-use product is destined for an entity that is owned, controlled, or linked to a sanctioned beneficial owner, the system automatically places a hold on the shipment.

By combining product-level AI with deep network analytics, compliance officers only need to manually review high-risk alerts. This integration prevents costly regulatory fines, protects national security, and ensures legitimate international trade continues without unnecessary delays.
