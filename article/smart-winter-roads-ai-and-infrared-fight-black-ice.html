Smart Winter Roads: AI and Infrared Fight Black Ice

Cold-region highway departments are revolutionizing winter road maintenance by combining road-surface infrared thermometry with artificial intelligence to optimize salt-brine application. This advanced approach allows agencies to accurately predict and prevent hazardous black ice formation in real time. By moving away from traditional, blanket salting methods and adopting data-driven precision spraying, transportation departments are maintaining safer roads while drastically reducing toxic chloride runoff into local freshwater streams.

The Role of Road-Surface Infrared Thermometry

Preventing black ice requires knowing the exact temperature of the asphalt, which often differs significantly from the ambient air temperature. Highway departments achieve this by mounting mobile infrared (IR) thermometers onto snowplows and patrol vehicles, alongside stationary Road Weather Information Systems (RWIS).

These non-contact sensors measure the thermal radiation emitting from the pavement as the vehicle drives at normal highway speeds. The sensors provide a continuous, real-time thermal profile of the road network, identifying microclimates, shaded areas, and bridge decks where pavement temperatures drop below freezing faster than surrounding areas.

AI-Driven Salt-Brine Optimization

Gathering temperature data is only the first step; artificial intelligence makes that data actionable. The AI software ingests the real-time infrared pavement temperatures and combines them with hyper-local weather forecasts, humidity levels, residual salt measurements on the road, and traffic density data.

Using machine learning algorithms, the system predicts the exact locations and times that moisture will freeze onto the pavement to create black ice. Instead of relying on human guesswork, the AI calculates the precise concentration and volume of salt brine—a liquid mixture of water and rock salt—needed to lower the freezing point of the water on that specific stretch of road.

This data is fed directly into automated spreader controllers on the trucks. As the truck moves, the AI adjusts the brine flow rate millisecond by millisecond, applying higher concentrations on freezing bridge decks and shutting off entirely on warmer stretches of pavement.

Minimizing Chloride Runoff into Streams

Traditional rock salt often bounces off the road during application or is blown away by traffic before it can melt ice, leading to massive over-application. When snow melts, this excess chloride washes into soil and nearby waterways, threatening aquatic ecosystems and contaminating drinking water.

The combination of infrared thermometry and AI directly combats this environmental crisis in three ways:

  • Pre-Wetting Efficiency: Liquid salt brine adheres to the pavement immediately upon contact, preventing bounce and scatter. It also begins working instantly, unlike solid salt which requires heat and moisture to dissolve first.
  • Micro-Dosing: Because the AI knows the exact thermal state of the road, it applies the minimum effective dose required to prevent ice bonding. If a road surface is dry or safely above freezing, the system deploys zero brine.
  • Anti-Icing Over De-Icing: By predicting black ice before it forms, departments can apply a thin layer of brine ahead of a freeze (anti-icing). Preventing ice from bonding to the pavement requires up to 75% less salt than trying to melt thick ice that has already formed (de-icing).

Through the integration of infrared sensing and artificial intelligence, highway departments are successfully balancing public safety with environmental stewardship, often cutting their total salt usage by 30% to 50% per winter season.
