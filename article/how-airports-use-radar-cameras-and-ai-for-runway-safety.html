How Airports Use Radar, Cameras, and AI for Runway Safety

International airports are increasingly deploying integrated networks of avian radar, millimeter-wave runway cameras, and artificial intelligence to prevent catastrophic bird strikes and eliminate foreign object debris (FOD). This article explores how these three advanced technologies work together in real-time to monitor airspace and tarmac conditions, verify hazards, and trigger automated responses that protect aircraft during critical takeoff and landing phases.

Avian Radar for Airspace Monitoring

Avian radar systems serve as the first line of defense by providing 360-degree, three-dimensional tracking of bird activity in and around the airport perimeter. Unlike traditional air traffic control radar, which filters out small targets, avian radar uses specialized S-band and X-band frequencies to detect single birds or large flocks up to several miles away.

These systems track the altitude, speed, and trajectory of birds in real-time. By monitoring migration patterns and local foraging movements, the radar establishes a continuous operational picture of the airspace, operating effectively even in darkness or heavy fog.

Millimeter-Wave Cameras for FOD Detection

While avian radar monitors the sky, millimeter-wave radar and high-resolution optical cameras continuously scan the physical runway surfaces for foreign object debris. FOD includes anything from fallen aircraft parts and luggage hardware to stones and tools, all of which can puncture tires or be ingested into jet engines.

Millimeter-wave sensors emit high-frequency electromagnetic waves that bounce off objects on the tarmac. Because millimeter waves can penetrate heavy rain, snow, fog, and dust, they successfully detect objects as small as a metal bolt from hundreds of meters away. When the millimeter-wave sensor detects an anomaly, paired optical and thermal cameras zoom in on the exact coordinates to capture a clear visual of the object.

Artificial Intelligence as the Central Brain

Sensor data alone can overwhelm human operators with false alarms caused by blowing grass, shadows, or harmless insects. Artificial intelligence acts as the central processor that unifies the avian radar and runway camera networks, turning raw data into actionable intelligence.

AI algorithms use computer vision and machine learning to analyze the fused data streams:

  • Classification: When a millimeter-wave sensor detects an object on the runway, AI analyzes the camera feed to determine if the object is dangerous FOD, a harmless shadow, or a stationary bird.
  • Predictive Tracking: By analyzing avian radar data alongside weather patterns, the AI predicts whether a flock of birds will intersect with an approaching aircraft's flight path.
  • Automated Deterrence: If the AI confirms a bird hazard in a critical zone, it can automatically trigger targeted acoustic hailing devices, lasers, or distress calls on the airfield to disperse the flock without human intervention.
  • Prioritized Alerts: When dangerous FOD is verified, the AI sends the exact GPS coordinates and an image of the debris to runway maintenance crews and air traffic control, allowing them to halt departures briefly and retrieve the item swiftly.

The Unified Safety Workflow

In a real-world scenario, these technologies operate as a single automated loop. If a bird drops a piece of debris onto the tarmac, the avian radar tracks the bird's flight path while the millimeter-wave sensor instantly flags the dropped item on the asphalt. The AI slews a runway camera to the location, identifies the object as a hazard, alerts the ground crew for retrieval, and updates air traffic control—all within seconds. This seamless integration drastically reduces runway closure times and prevents millions of dollars in aircraft damage.
