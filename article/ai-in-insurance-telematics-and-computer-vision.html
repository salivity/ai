AI in Insurance: Telematics and Computer Vision

This article explores how modern insurance companies utilize telematics and computer vision artificial intelligence to revolutionize the industry. By leveraging real-time behavioral data from connected devices, insurers can move away from generalized demographics to accurately price individualized risk. Simultaneously, advancements in computer vision allow carriers to automate property and auto claims assessments, drastically reducing processing times, minimizing human error, and lowering operational costs.

Pricing Individualized Risk with Telematics

Historically, auto and property insurers relied on broad demographic proxies—such as age, gender, credit score, and zip code—to estimate the likelihood of a claim. Today, telematics technology allows insurers to price policies based on actual behavior and usage.

Telematics involves gathering data through smartphone apps, onboard diagnostics (OBD-II) devices, or built-in vehicle sensors. These systems continuously track and transmit specific driving metrics, including:

  • Hard braking and rapid acceleration: Indicators of aggressive driving.
  • Cornering and speed: Measurements of how safely a driver navigates roads and adheres to speed limits.
  • Time of day and location: Data showing how often a vehicle is driven during high-risk hours, such as late at night, or in congested areas.
  • Total mileage: The exact distance driven, which directly correlates to exposure to potential accidents.

Artificial intelligence algorithms process this vast influx of raw sensor data to generate an individualized risk score for the driver. Through Usage-Based Insurance (UBI) models like Pay-How-You-Drive (PHYD) and Pay-As-You-Drive (PAYD), safe and low-mileage drivers are rewarded with lower premiums, while riskier drivers pay rates that reflect their actual on-road habits.

In the property insurance sector, a similar approach is taken using Internet of Things (IoT) sensors. Smart home devices monitor water pressure, temperature fluctuations, and electrical systems to detect leaks or fire hazards early, allowing insurers to offer discounts to proactive homeowners and price policies based on the real-time condition of the property.

Automating Claims Assessment with Computer Vision

When an accident or property damage occurs, the traditional claims process requires manual inspections by human adjusters, leading to weeks of delays. Computer vision, a subset of AI that enables computers to interpret and make decisions based on visual data, automates this workflow.

Auto Claims Processing

Following a vehicular collision, policyholders can use their smartphones to capture photos or videos of the damage and upload them directly to the insurer's app. Computer vision models analyze these images in seconds through a multi-step process:

  1. Damage Detection: The AI identifies the exact vehicle make and model, pinpoints the damaged parts (e.g., bumper, fender, windshield), and classifies the type of damage (e.g., dent, scratch, structural).
  2. Severity Assessment: Deep learning algorithms, trained on millions of historical accident photos, determine whether a part needs a simple repair or a complete replacement.
  3. Cost Estimation: The system cross-references the required repairs with real-time databases of local labor rates and OEM part prices to generate an immediate repair estimate.

For minor to moderate accidents, this allows insurers to approve claims and issue payouts within minutes of the submission, bypassing the need for an in-person adjuster.

Property Claims and Pre-Underwriting

Computer vision is equally transformative in homeowners and commercial property insurance. Insurers apply AI to high-resolution aerial imagery captured by satellites, drones, and fixed-wing aircraft.

Before a policy is even written, computer vision scans a property's roof to detect existing wear and tear, missing shingles, overhanging tree branches, or undeclared hazards like trampolines and swimming pools. This ensures accurate underwriting without sending an inspector to the home.

After a catastrophic event, such as a hurricane or wildfire, drones and satellites capture images of the affected region. Computer vision algorithms compare these post-event images with pre-event baselines to automatically categorize the severity of structural damage across thousands of properties simultaneously. This enables insurers to triage claims, dispatch emergency funds, and allocate human adjusters to the most severe cases immediately.

Fraud Detection

Beyond speed and accuracy, computer vision acts as a safeguard against insurance fraud. When photos are submitted for a claim, AI analyzes the image metadata to verify the time and location of the incident. Furthermore, the system scans the visual data for signs of digital manipulation and cross-references the images against industry-wide databases to ensure the exact same damage photos were not previously used in an older claim.
