Neural Network NMPC in Distillation and Air Separation

Nonlinear Model Predictive Control (NMPC) augmented with neural networks is revolutionizing the automation of cryogenic air separation units (ASUs) and chemical distillation columns. By merging traditional physics-based optimization with data-driven artificial intelligence, this hybrid control architecture overcomes the computational bottlenecks and inaccuracies of standard controllers. This article explores how this advanced approach predicts complex fluid dynamics in real-time, maintains precise product purity, prevents system instabilities, and reduces energy consumption across extreme operational environments.

The Challenge of Controlling Separation Columns

Cryogenic air separation and chemical distillation columns are among the most energy-intensive and complex systems in industrial engineering. They rely on precise thermal and fluid dynamics to separate mixtures into pure components based on boiling points. Controlling these columns is notoriously difficult due to three main factors:

  • High Nonlinearity: Small changes in reflux ratios or reboiler heat duties can cause disproportionate, non-linear shifts in product purity.
  • Variable Coupling: Adjusting one parameter, such as pressure, simultaneously affects temperature profiles and flow rates throughout the entire column.
  • Long Dead Times: A disturbance in the feed composition may take hours to manifest in the final product stream, making reactive control strategies ineffective.

Traditional Proportional-Integral-Derivative (PID) controllers and standard Linear Model Predictive Control (LMPC) struggle when these columns operate outside narrow, steady-state conditions.

The Bottleneck of Traditional NMPC

Standard NMPC addresses nonlinearity by using rigorous first-principles models—equations based on mass, energy, and momentum balances—to predict how the column will behave in the future. The controller solves an optimization problem at every time step to determine the best control actions.

However, a rigorous mathematical model of a multi-stage distillation column involves thousands of differential-algebraic equations. Solving these equations in real-time requires immense computational power. Often, the physical process changes faster than the computer can calculate the next optimal move, leading to control delays and instability.

How Neural Networks Augment NMPC

Integrating artificial intelligence, specifically deep neural networks (DNNs) or recurrent neural networks (RNNs), solves the computational bottleneck of NMPC. Engineers use neural networks in two primary ways: as surrogate models or as residual learners.

In surrogate modeling, the neural network is trained on historical plant data and high-fidelity simulation data. Once trained, it replaces the heavy physics equations entirely. Because a neural network processes inputs to outputs using simple matrix multiplication, it can predict future column states in milliseconds rather than minutes.

In hybrid residual modeling, a simplified physics model is paired with a neural network. The physics model handles the basic mass and energy balances, while the neural network learns the unmodeled dynamics—such as tray efficiency degradation, foaming, or ambient heat loss. This guarantees the AI respects the laws of thermodynamics while remaining fast and accurate.

Stabilizing Cryogenic Air Separation Units

Cryogenic ASUs separate air into nitrogen, oxygen, and argon at extremely low temperatures. These plants frequently ramp production up or down to take advantage of fluctuating electricity prices, which pushes the columns into unstable transient states.

Neural-network-augmented NMPC stabilizes ASUs by accurately predicting the behavior of the Argon side-column, which is highly sensitive to composition shifts. The AI-driven controller anticipates how changes in air feed flow will impact the cryogenic heat exchangers and distillation trays. It proactively adjusts expansion turbines and valve positions to prevent "dumping" (liquid falling through tray perforations) and "flooding" (liquid backing up the column), maintaining ultra-high purity oxygen and nitrogen even during rapid load changes.

Stabilizing Chemical Distillation Columns

In chemical and petrochemical refining, distillation columns frequently experience feed disturbances, such as sudden changes in crude oil composition or upstream reactor fluctuations.

When a feed disturbance occurs, the neural network processes sensor data (temperatures, pressures, and flow rates) to infer real-time chemical compositions without waiting for slow gas chromatograph analyzers. The NMPC then calculates the exact reboiler heat duty and reflux flow adjustments needed to counteract the disturbance before it ruins the product batch. By keeping the column stable and close to its operational constraints without crossing safety boundaries, the controller minimizes over-purification, which directly reduces steam and cooling water consumption.
