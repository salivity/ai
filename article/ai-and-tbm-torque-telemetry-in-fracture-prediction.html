AI and TBM Torque Telemetry in Fracture Prediction

Tunnel Boring Machines (TBMs) utilize real-time cutterhead torque telemetry combined with artificial intelligence (AI) to predict geological hazards before they are excavated. By analyzing the mechanical resistance encountered at the tunnel face, AI algorithms can identify sudden changes in rock mass integrity, such as fractures and fault zones. This predictive capability allows operators to adjust machine parameters immediately, stabilizing the ground and preventing catastrophic collapses ahead of the TBM.

Understanding Cutterhead Torque Telemetry

Cutterhead torque measures the rotational force required to grind through subterranean rock and soil. As the TBM advances, sensors continuously record this torque alongside thrust force, penetration rate, and rotation speed. This stream of data is known as telemetry.

When the cutterhead encounters homogenous, solid rock, the torque remains relatively high and stable. However, when the machine approaches or enters fractured rock masses, the telemetry changes instantly. Fractured rock offers inconsistent resistance, causing erratic spikes and drops in torque as the cutting discs catch on broken rock blocks or slip into voids.

The Role of Artificial Intelligence

Human operators cannot manually interpret the thousands of telemetry data points generated every second to predict what lies ahead. AI bridges this gap through machine learning models trained on historical tunneling data and geological surveys.

The AI processes the live torque telemetry using the following steps:

  • Noise Reduction: The system filters out standard operational vibrations to isolate true geological signals.
  • Pattern Recognition: Deep learning algorithms, such as Long Short-Term Memory (LSTM) networks, analyze the time-series data from the cutterhead. They match current torque fluctuations against known signatures of rock fractures, shear zones, and water-bearing faults.
  • Spatial Forecasting: By correlating the rate of change in torque with the TBM's advance rate, the AI extrapolates the data to map the rock mass integrity several meters ahead of the cutterhead.

Preventing Ground Collapse

Once the AI predicts a fracture zone ahead of the tunnel face, the system initiates preventive measures, either automatically or by alerting the operator. Preventing a collapse relies on balancing the pressure at the excavation face.

  1. Adjusting Operational Parameters: The TBM reduces its thrust and cutterhead rotation speed. This minimizes mechanical disturbance to the already weakened rock mass ahead.
  2. Regulating Face Pressure: In shielded TBMs, the system adjusts the pressure of the excavated material or slurry in the chamber behind the cutterhead. Maintaining exact counter-pressure supports the fractured rock, keeping it from caving in.
  3. Proactive Ground Conditioning: If severe fractures are predicted, the TBM can halt advancement to inject polyurethane resins or cement grout ahead of the face. This consolidates the broken rock into a stable mass before the cutterhead arrives.

By converting mechanical stress data into geological forecasts, the integration of torque telemetry and AI transforms the cutterhead into a giant sensor. This technology ensures that TBMs can navigate complex underground environments safely without triggering ground collapses.
