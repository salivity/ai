China's Regulation of Generative AI Services

China has implemented one of the world’s first comprehensive regulatory frameworks for generative artificial intelligence, primarily enforced by the Cyberspace Administration of China (CAC). Through the "Interim Measures for the Management of Generative Artificial Intelligence Services," the government sets strict guidelines for public-facing AI tools. This article provides a clear breakdown of how China regulates these services across three core areas: strict content controls aligned with state ideology, mandatory algorithm registration, and rigorous standards for training data.

Content Controls

Content regulation is the foundation of China's approach to generative AI. Service providers are legally responsible for the outputs produced by their platforms and must function as information content producers under existing censorship laws.

  • Ideological Alignment: AI-generated content must adhere to "Core Socialist Values." It is strictly prohibited from generating material that incites subversion of state power, undermines national unity, or damages the national image.
  • Prohibited Material: Providers must block the generation of violence, obscenity, terrorism, false information, and content that promotes ethnic discrimination or economic disruption.
  • Prompt Intervention: Companies must actively monitor user inputs. If a user attempts to generate illegal content, the provider must suspend or terminate their access. If illegal content is generated, the provider must immediately stop its transmission, remove the data, and report the incident to authorities.
  • Content Labeling: All content created by generative AI—especially deepfakes, images, and videos—must be clearly watermarked or labeled to prevent public confusion.

Algorithm Registration

Before a generative AI service with "public opinion attributes or social mobilization capabilities" can be offered to the Chinese public, it must pass rigorous bureaucratic hurdles.

  • The Algorithm Registry: Providers must file their algorithms with the CAC's centralized registry. This submission includes technical details about how the algorithm works, the data it uses, and its intended application.
  • Security Assessments: Alongside registration, companies must conduct and submit a formal security assessment. Regulators use this to evaluate the model's potential to influence public opinion or pose national security risks.
  • Targeted Scope: These rules apply specifically to public-facing services. Research institutions and enterprises developing generative AI for internal use are generally exempt from registration, provided they do not release the tools to the general public.

Training Data Standards

To ensure that AI outputs are safe, accurate, and controllable, the Chinese government strictly regulates the inputs used to build the models.

  • Lawful Sourcing: Service providers must use data and foundational models from legitimate sources. The regulations explicitly prohibit the use of training data that infringes on intellectual property rights.
  • Privacy Protection: If training data includes personal information, providers must obtain the consent of the individuals involved or ensure the data complies with China’s Personal Information Protection Law (PIPL).
  • Data Quality and Truthfulness: Providers are required to take effective measures to improve the quality, authenticity, accuracy, and objectivity of their training data. This is mandated to prevent the AI from generating hallucinations, falsehoods, or biases based on nationality, gender, or age.
  • Labor Protections: The regulations also mandate that companies train and fairly manage the human annotators who label data during the AI development process.
