AI Reinforcement Learning in Autonomous Greenhouses

Autonomous commercial greenhouses are revolutionizing agriculture by using reinforcement learning (RL) to optimize crop yields and resource efficiency. This article explores how RL algorithms dynamically balance supplemental LED lighting, carbon dioxide (CO2) dosing, and transpiration humidity. By continuously learning from sensor data and crop responses, the AI creates an ideal microclimate that maximizes photosynthesis while minimizing energy and resource waste.

Understanding Reinforcement Learning in Agriculture

Reinforcement learning is a type of machine learning where an AI agent learns to make decisions by performing actions and receiving feedback in the form of rewards or penalties. In a commercial greenhouse, the AI agent controls the climate actuators. The environment is the greenhouse itself, and the reward is a mathematical calculation combining crop growth rate, fruit quality, and resource conservation. Unlike traditional rule-based systems that rely on static thresholds, RL adapts in real-time to unpredictable external weather and the changing physiological needs of the plants.

Optimizing Supplemental LED Lighting

Plants require a specific Daily Light Integral (DLI) to thrive. However, artificial lighting is one of the highest operational costs in a greenhouse. The RL algorithm manages LED lighting by analyzing weather forecasts, real-time solar radiation, and dynamic electricity pricing.

If the AI predicts a sunny afternoon, it reduces LED output in the morning to save energy, knowing the sun will fulfill the DLI. Conversely, during overcast days, the AI increases LED intensity, specifically tuning the light spectrum (such as red and blue wavelengths) to match the exact growth stage of the crop. The algorithm only applies light when the plant has enough CO2 and optimal humidity to actually process the photons, preventing wasted electricity.

Precision CO2 Dosing

Carbon dioxide is essential for photosynthesis, and enriching the greenhouse air with CO2 significantly boosts plant growth. However, CO2 quickly escapes when roof vents are opened for cooling or dehumidification.

The RL system balances CO2 dosing by syncing it directly with the lighting and ventilation schedules. When LED or natural light levels are high, the AI injects more CO2 to maximize the photosynthetic rate. If the system anticipates that vents will need to open soon to release excess heat, the AI halts CO2 dosing ahead of time so the gas is not immediately vented into the outside atmosphere.

Managing Transpiration and Humidity

As plants absorb light and CO2, they transpire—releasing water vapor into the air through their leaves. This process is vital for moving nutrients from the roots to the rest of the plant. However, excess transpiration leads to high humidity, which stunts further transpiration and invites fungal diseases like botrytis.

The RL agent manages this by targeting the optimal Vapor Pressure Deficit (VPD), which is the difference between the moisture in the air and how much moisture the air can hold when saturated. The AI monitors plant temperature and ambient humidity to keep the VPD in a sweet spot. If humidity rises too high due to heavy transpiration under intense LED lighting, the AI must make a complex choice: open the vents (losing heat and CO2) or engage mechanical dehumidifiers (using electricity). The RL algorithm calculates the exact cost-benefit of each action and executes the most efficient option.

The Balancing Act: How the AI Connects All Three

The true power of reinforcement learning lies in managing the interdependence of light, CO2, and humidity simultaneously. Changing one variable instantly affects the others.

  1. Data Ingestion: The AI reads current state metrics, including indoor PAR (Photosynthetically Active Radiation) light levels, CO2 parts per million (ppm), relative humidity, leaf temperature, and outside weather.
  2. Simulation and Action: When the AI turns on the LED lights, it anticipates a spike in plant transpiration. Before the humidity can reach dangerous levels, the AI proactively adjusts heating pipes and dehumidifiers to maintain the correct VPD.
  3. Coordinated Dosing: Simultaneously, because the stomata (pores) on the leaves are fully open due to the ideal humidity and light, the AI injects a precise dose of CO2. The plants absorb the maximum amount of carbon with minimal waste.
  4. Reward Evaluation: The system measures the resulting biomass increase against the cost of the electricity and CO2 used. Over thousands of cycles, the AI refines this balancing act, discovering microclimate strategies that human growers might never consider.
