Secure Multi-Party Computation for Privacy-Preserving AI

Secure Multi-Party Computation (SMPC) is a cryptographic technique that allows competing organizations, such as hospitals and banks, to jointly train and run artificial intelligence models without ever exposing their private data to one another. By splitting sensitive data into encrypted fragments and distributing them among the participants, these entities can compute collective results—like detecting complex financial fraud or identifying rare diseases—while keeping their underlying proprietary datasets completely hidden. This article explains the mechanics of SMPC, how it enables secure AI collaboration, and its practical applications in healthcare and finance.

The Core Mechanism: Secret Sharing

At the heart of SMPC is a concept known as "secret sharing." Instead of sending raw data to a central server, an organization breaks its sensitive information into multiple cryptographic pieces, called shares.

On their own, these individual shares look like random, meaningless numbers. To understand how this works, imagine a simple calculation where three banks want to find the average of their total fraud losses without revealing their specific numbers:

  1. Fragmentation: Bank A takes its actual loss number and splits it into three random numbers that add up to the original total.
  2. Distribution: Bank A keeps one piece and sends the other two pieces to Bank B and Bank C.
  3. Computation: Bank B and Bank C do the exact same thing with their own numbers. Each bank then adds up the random pieces they currently hold.
  4. Reconstruction: The banks share the sums of their random pieces and add those together. The final total is mathematically identical to the sum of the original secret numbers, yet no bank ever saw the others' private inputs.

Applying SMPC to Artificial Intelligence

Training an AI model is fundamentally a series of complex mathematical operations, primarily additions and multiplications of matrices. Because SMPC allows parties to perform math on encrypted shares, organizations can execute the entire machine learning lifecycle without decrypting the underlying data.

When competing organizations train an AI model using SMPC, they follow a strict protocol:

  • Data Preparation: Each institution formats its local data (such as patient records or transaction logs) and converts the values into cryptographic shares.
  • Distributed Training: The shares are distributed among the participating servers. The AI algorithm runs directly on these shares. As the model adjusts its internal weights and learns patterns, the updates themselves remain split into secret shares.
  • Private Output: Once training is complete, the participants combine their shares of the final AI model. Each participant receives the fully trained, highly accurate AI model, having learned nothing about the specific data points contributed by their competitors.

Use Case 1: Competing Hospitals

Hospitals hold vast amounts of valuable medical data, but strict privacy laws like HIPAA and competitive pressures prevent them from sharing patient records. However, an AI trained on data from a single hospital may fail to recognize rare diseases or might be biased toward local demographics.

Using SMPC, multiple competing hospitals can pool their data virtually. They can jointly train diagnostic AI models on millions of combined patient records to predict cancer outcomes or identify rare genetic disorders. Throughout the entire process, the patient data never leaves the control of the originating hospital in a readable format, ensuring 100% regulatory compliance and protecting institutional privacy.

Use Case 2: Competing Banks

In the financial sector, money launderers and fraudsters often exploit the lack of communication between competing banks by moving illicit funds across different institutions. A single bank only sees a small piece of the puzzle, making it difficult for their internal AI to flag sophisticated criminal networks.

With SMPC, competing banks can run joint AI inference and training across their combined transaction histories. The AI can trace money flows across institutions and identify complex fraud patterns. The banks receive actionable intelligence and superior fraud-detection models while keeping their customer lists, proprietary trading strategies, and transaction volumes completely confidential.

Challenges to Consider

While SMPC provides mathematical guarantees of privacy, it introduces new operational challenges. Because every mathematical operation in an AI model requires participants to exchange encrypted shares back and forth, SMPC generates heavy network traffic. This communication overhead makes training AI via SMPC significantly slower and more computationally expensive than standard, unencrypted training. Consequently, organizations must balance the absolute privacy of SMPC with the high bandwidth and processing power required to execute it.
