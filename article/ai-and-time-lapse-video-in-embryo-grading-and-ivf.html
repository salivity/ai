AI and Time-Lapse Video in Embryo Grading and IVF

Reproductive medicine clinics are transforming in vitro fertilization (IVF) by combining time-lapse incubator video with computer vision artificial intelligence (AI). This article provides an overview of how fertility specialists use continuous embryo monitoring and deep learning algorithms to non-invasively predict blastocyst chromosomal normalcy (ploidy) and rank embryos by their exact implantation potential, leading to higher pregnancy rates and fewer miscarriages.

The Role of Time-Lapse Incubators

Traditionally, embryologists had to remove developing embryos from their incubators once a day to examine them under a microscope. This process exposed the fragile embryos to fluctuations in temperature, pH, and oxygen levels, while only providing static snapshots of development.

Time-lapse incubators solve this problem by integrating built-in microscopes and cameras directly into the incubation chamber. These systems capture images of the developing embryos every 5 to 20 minutes over five to six days. When stitched together, these images create a continuous video of the embryo’s journey from fertilization to the blastocyst stage. This uninterrupted footage reveals morphokinetics—the precise timing and dynamic patterns of cell division—while keeping the embryo in an undisturbed, optimal environment.

How Computer Vision AI Analyzes Embryos

A single time-lapse video of an embryo contains thousands of data points, which is far too much information for a human embryologist to analyze manually. Clinics use computer vision, a branch of artificial intelligence, to process these complex visual datasets.

The AI systems are built using deep learning and convolutional neural networks (CNNs). Developers train these algorithms on hundreds of thousands of historical time-lapse videos with known clinical outcomes—such as whether the embryo had normal chromosomes or resulted in a live birth. By analyzing pixel-level changes across thousands of frames, the computer vision software detects microscopic morphological traits and subtle kinetic patterns that are invisible to the human eye. This removes human subjectivity, ensuring that an embryo is graded the exact same way regardless of the clinic or the embryologist on duty.

Non-Invasive Ploidy Prediction

Ploidy refers to the number of chromosomes in a cell. A "euploid" embryo has the correct number of chromosomes and a high chance of leading to a healthy baby, while an "aneuploid" embryo has missing or extra chromosomes, which usually results in failed implantation or miscarriage.

Historically, determining ploidy required Preimplantation Genetic Testing for Aneuploidy (PGT-A). This is an invasive, expensive biopsy where a few cells are physically removed from the blastocyst’s outer layer for genetic sequencing.

Today, AI and time-lapse video offer a non-invasive alternative. Aneuploid embryos divide differently than euploid embryos. Computer vision AI tracks the exact timing of early cell divisions, the symmetry of the cells, and the speed of blastocyst expansion. By identifying irregularities in these cellular rhythms, the AI assigns a probability score indicating whether the blastocyst is chromosomally normal. While it does not always replace genetic biopsies in high-risk cases, it allows clinics to prioritize likely euploid embryos without the cost or physical trauma of a biopsy.

Grading Implantation Potential

Even if an embryo has normal chromosomes, it is not guaranteed to implant in the uterus. To grade implantation potential, the AI evaluates the mature blastocyst on day five or six of development.

The computer vision model automatically segments and measures specific parts of the blastocyst:

  • The Inner Cell Mass (ICM): The cluster of cells that will eventually form the fetus.
  • The Trophectoderm: The outer layer of cells that will form the placenta and must successfully attach to the uterine wall.
  • The Blastocoel: The fluid-filled cavity that helps the embryo expand and hatch.

By combining the visual quality of these structures with the morphokinetic data from the previous five days, the AI generates an objective implantation score, often on a scale of 1 to 10. This score directly correlates with the likelihood of the embryo achieving a clinical pregnancy.

Clinical Benefits for Patients

By merging time-lapse video with AI grading, reproductive medicine clinics can accurately identify the single best embryo for transfer. This empowers doctors to confidently perform Single Embryo Transfers (SET), which virtually eliminates the medical risks associated with twin or triplet pregnancies. Ultimately, this technology shortens the time it takes for patients to get pregnant, reduces the emotional and financial toll of failed IVF cycles, and standardizes care across the fertility industry.
