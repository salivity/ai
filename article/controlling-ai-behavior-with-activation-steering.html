Controlling AI Behavior with Activation Steering

Activation steering is an advanced technique used to control the behavior of artificial intelligence models at runtime without modifying their underlying weights. By directly intervening in a model's internal residual streams during the forward pass, developers can guide the AI toward specific traits, topics, or safety constraints. This article explores the mechanics of residual streams, how steering vectors are found, and how they are injected into the model to dynamically alter its outputs in real time.

Understanding the Residual Stream

To understand activation steering, it is first necessary to understand the residual stream of a transformer model. As an AI processes an input prompt, the data flows through a series of layers consisting of attention mechanisms and multi-layer perceptrons (MLPs).

The residual stream acts as a central communication highway running through the entire network. Each layer reads information from this stream, processes it, and writes its output back into the stream by adding new vectors to the existing ones. The state of the residual stream at any given layer is a high-dimensional vector representing the model's current understanding and intentions for the next generated token.

Extracting the Steering Vector

Before a model can be steered, engineers must identify the specific direction in the high-dimensional space that corresponds to a desired behavior, such as honesty, sycophancy, or a specific writing style. This direction is known as a steering vector.

The most common method for finding a steering vector is contrastive activation addition. This involves feeding the model pairs of prompts that represent opposite behaviors. For example, to find an "honesty" vector, researchers feed the model prompts that encourage truthful answers alongside prompts that encourage deceptive answers. As the model processes both sets of prompts, researchers record the internal activations within the residual stream at a specific layer. By subtracting the average activations of the deceptive prompts from the average activations of the truthful prompts, they isolate the vector that represents the concept of honesty.

Modifying the Stream at Runtime

Once the steering vector is extracted, it can be used to control the model during live inference. When a user submits a prompt, the model begins its standard forward pass, passing data down the residual stream from layer to layer.

At a predetermined layer—usually in the middle of the network where abstract concepts are formed—the system intercepts the residual stream. The pre-computed steering vector is multiplied by a chosen scalar value (a coefficient that determines the strength of the intervention) and added directly to the current activation vector in the residual stream.

After this addition is made, the modified residual stream is passed on to the next layer, and the model continues its forward pass normally. Because the internal state now contains a stronger mathematical representation of the desired behavior, the remaining layers process the information through that lens, resulting in a final output that aligns with the steered direction.

Advantages Over Traditional Methods

Modifying the residual stream at runtime offers significant benefits over traditional AI control methods like fine-tuning and prompt engineering.

  • Zero Weight Modification: Fine-tuning requires computationally expensive updates to the model's permanent weights and risks degrading its general capabilities. Activation steering leaves the base model entirely intact.
  • Dynamic Adjustability: By simply changing the scalar multiplier of the steering vector, developers can dial a behavior up, dial it down, or even invert it (by using a negative multiplier) on a per-request basis.
  • Prompt Independence: Unlike system prompts, which consume context window tokens and can be bypassed by clever user "jailbreaks," activation steering operates directly on the model's internal cognition, making it much harder for users to override.
